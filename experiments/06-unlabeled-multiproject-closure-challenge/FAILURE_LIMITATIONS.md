# Failures and Limitations

## Frozen failure rule

After the first scored held-out run, no policy semantics, structural inference, fixture, project/version manifest, training input, held-out input, or checkpoint protocol may change to improve the result. Every miss must remain published and minimized. Later changes are limited to explicitly receipted evidence, documentation, test, or packaging defects.

## Known limitations before scoring

1. The challenge covers three canonical repository subtrees at one base commit, not arbitrary projects or organic histories.
2. The in-memory mutations are designed research fixtures. They are not called separate real projects.
3. Structural audit uses public contract parameters. A helper with neither traceable reads nor useful ordinary contract shape may remain invisible.
4. Checkpoint reconstruction assumes the canonical subtree itself remains available and verified. Without it, the protocol abstains/escalates instead of claiming equality.
5. The full offline scoring oracle is still necessary to count hidden corruption; its answers never route or repair.
6. Checks are deterministic and side-effect free. Effectful or hostile evaluators are not tested.
7. Byte counts use Python object estimates and canonical serialized sizes. They are not process-isolated memory measurements.
8. Wall and CPU time are host-specific observations.
9. Greedy minimization proves a retained reproduction under this mutation language, not a globally minimal semantic cause.
10. No component novelty, production reliability, security, AI, model-weight, substrate, brain, physics, or domain-actuator claim is made.
